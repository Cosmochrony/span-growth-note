\begin{abstract}
The spectral admissibility programme measures a projective block capacity $\sigma_c(n)$ along the
breadth-first cascade of the Heisenberg Cayley graph and converts capacity exponents into cascade growth
exponents through the structural relation $\beta = 1/(\delta + \tfrac{1}{2})$, derived on
Ramanujan--Lubotzky--Phillips--Sarnak (LPS) relaxation graphs.
The present note establishes the span-growth law that the Heisenberg substrate itself imposes, states its
rigorous status, and proves that the expander-derived conversion law does not transfer to the substrate on
which the capacities are measured.
The cumulative fingerprint span $r(n)$ obeys the exact increment identity $\Delta r(n) = \sigma_c(n)\,|S_n|$,
and the exact breadth-first spheres of $\Heis$ grow polynomially with homogeneous dimension $D = 4$.
For real-valued weight sequences with power-law decay, summation yields a complete three-branch
classification depending only on $(\dc, D)$: polynomial span growth $r(n) \sim n^{D-\dc}$ for $\dc < D$,
logarithmic growth at $\dc = D$, saturation for $\dc > D$.
Because the measured increments are integers, the pointwise power-law hypothesis is unrealizable whenever
$\dc > D - 1$ --- a range that includes the measured windows --- and the classification reaches the measured
rank only through an exact finite-window formulation with two-sided bounds; every numerical exponent
statement is window-scoped, and no joint large-$q$ limit is claimed.
The transfer no-go has three independent legs, each scoped to the constructions the corpus defines.
Valence--exploration proportionality forces a vanishing capacity exponent and fails for both native
realisations of the valence --- outright at fixed $q$ by the dimension count $r \le q$ against
$|B_n| \to q^3$.
The native frontier is a power of the explored volume, $|S_n| \asymp |B_n|^{3/4}$, not of the achieved span:
no uniform constant can restore the square-root frontier form for $D > 2$, so the additive $\tfrac12$ is
expander-specific, and the bounded-flux constant acquires no native carrier because the explored volume is
autonomous on a fixed graph.
Finally, the pair observable $\sigma_{\mathrm{pair}} = \sigma_c\sigma_{q-c}$ carries no defined native growth
process, and the exponent coordinates of the conversion law ($\alpha$ against valence, $\delta$ against shell
index) coincide only in the reduced filling model, which fails natively; the general
separation-of-variables algebra is itself coordinate-honest and returns the native law $\beta = D - \dc$ on
the growth branch.
Two consequences for the block-level analysis follow: the shell-growth factor $D - 1$ is the native boundary
term, entering additively and never inside a reciprocal, while intra-window slopes are invariant under
constant normalisations; and fitting a power law against the shifted logarithm $\log(n{+}1)$ inflates the
exponent by a computable $8$--$13\%$ on the production windows.
Window-effective exponents on the production data make contact near $q = 211$ and separate strictly for
$q \ge 307$, a sign stable across all window-shift and knee-trimming variants and both logarithmic
conventions; the contact is transitional in the accessible data and the asymptotic value of $\dc$ remains
open.
The polynomial span exponent is distinct from the exponential per-shell rate carried by the symbol $\bstar$
in the projected-Yukawa mass factorisation; the two objects are separated explicitly.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Interpretive status.}
The classification and the no-go results are proved under explicit, separately stated hypotheses; the
numerical exponents are finite-window measurements; nothing here modifies a capacity measurement.
As a structural reading, the growth of resolved relational structure on an emergent substrate is a
competition between geometric opening (volume growth) and novelty exhaustion (capacity decay), and a
conversion law between the two is a property of the substrate's geometry, not of the cascade alone: changing
the geometry changes the law.
This reading is an interpretation, not a result.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\subsection{Position in the programme}
\label{ssec:position}

The spectral admissibility sub-programme of Cosmochrony constrains a cascade growth exponent $\beta$ through
the structural relation
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:lps-law}
  \beta \;=\; \frac{1}{\delta + \tfrac{1}{2}},
\end{equation}
derived in O6~\cite{Beau2026a10} --- there in the form $\beta = 1/(\alpha + \tfrac{1}{2})$ with $\alpha$ the
redundancy exponent in the valence, $R \sim p^{-\alpha}$ --- from a growth inequality on Ramanujan--LPS
relaxation graphs~\cite{LPS88} established in O4~\cite{Beau2026a8}, recast in capacity language in
O7~\cite{Beau2026a11}, and applied at the fibre level, in the $\delta$-form above, to the measured
pair-capacity exponent $\delta_{\mathrm{pair}}$ of the Heisenberg cascade in
O16--O24~\cite{Beau2026a20,Beau2026a25,Beau2026a26,Beau2026a27,Beau2026a28}.
The capacity measurements themselves~\cite{Beau2026a16,Beau2026a29} are performed on the Cayley graphs of
$\Heisq$, whose breadth-first (BFS) geometry is polynomial --- homogeneous dimension
$\Dhom = 4$~\cite{Bass1972,Guivarch1973} --- and therefore very different from the expander geometry on which
relation~\eqref{eq:lps-law} was derived.

This note asks the substrate's own question: \emph{what growth law does the Heisenberg cascade itself
impose}, using only objects native to a single Heisenberg BFS, with no LPS import?

\subsection{Result}
\label{ssec:result}

The answer is a complete and elementary classification, together with a three-legged no-go for the transfer
of relation~\eqref{eq:lps-law} to the measurement substrate.
The cumulative fingerprint span $r(n)$ --- the number of independent relational directions resolved up to
BFS depth $n$ --- satisfies the exact increment identity $\Delta r(n) = \sigma_c(n)\,|S_n|$, where
$\sigma_c$ is the per-shell block capacity and $|S_n|$ the BFS sphere.
For real-valued weight sequences with power-law decay $\sigma_c(n) \sim a\,n^{-\dc}$ and the exact
polynomial sphere growth of $\Heis$, summation gives (Theorem~\ref{thm:classification}):
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:branches}
  \begin{array}{ll}
    \dc < D: & r(n) \sim \dfrac{a\kappa}{D-\dc}\, n^{\,D-\dc},\\[3mm]
    \dc = D: & r(n) \sim a\kappa\, \log n,\\[2mm]
    \dc > D: & r_\infty - r(n) \sim \dfrac{a\kappa}{\dc-D}\, n^{-(\dc-D)}.
  \end{array}
\end{equation}
Saturation begins strictly at $\dc > D$; the capacity-to-growth map on the growth branch is
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:heis-law}
  \beta_{\mathrm{Heis}} \;=\; D - \dc \qquad (\dc < D),
\end{equation}
and is not of the form~\eqref{eq:lps-law}.
The rank $\Delta r(n)$ is integer-valued, and for $\dc > D - 1$ --- a range that includes the measured
windows --- no integer sequence can realise the pointwise power-law hypothesis
(Remark~\ref{rem:integrality}); the classification therefore reaches the measured rank only through the
exact finite-window formulation of Proposition~\ref{prop:window}, and every numerical exponent statement in
this note is window-scoped.
The transfer no-go consists of three independent statements, each scoped to the constructions the corpus
defines.
Valence--exploration proportionality forces a vanishing capacity exponent
(Proposition~\ref{prop:t1}) and fails for both native realisations of the LPS valence --- at fixed $q$
outright, by the dimension count $r(n) \le q$ against $|B_n| \to q^3$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:dimcount}).
The native frontier is a power of the explored volume, $|S_n| \asymp |B_n|^{3/4}$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:theta}), not of the achieved span: no uniform constant sustains
$\Delta r \le c\,\sigma_c\sqrt{r}$ for $D > 2$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:t3}), and the bounded-flux constant
$\cBI$ has no native carrier because the explored volume is autonomous on a fixed graph
(Remark~\ref{rem:bi}).
The pair observable carries no defined native growth process, and the exponent coordinates of the
conversion law coincide only in the reduced filling model, which fails natively
(Section~\ref{sec:obstructions}); the separation-of-variables algebra itself is coordinate-honest and
returns~\eqref{eq:heis-law} (Remark~\ref{rem:selfconsistency}).
Consequences for the block-level analysis --- the native placement of the shell-growth factor $D - 1$ and
the quantified estimator inflation of the shifted-logarithm convention --- are collected in
Section~\ref{sec:o14}.

\subsection{What this note does not claim}
\label{ssec:scope}

The note does not modify any capacity measurement, does not touch the shell-alignment theorem of
O22~\cite{Beau2026a26}, the threshold derivation of O23~\cite{Beau2026a27}, or the verticality result of
O24~\cite{Beau2026a28}, and makes no statement about the numerical value of any asymptotic exponent.
It establishes in which geometric regime the conversion law~\eqref{eq:lps-law} can be derived, and what
replaces it on the substrate where the capacities are actually measured.
The no-go statements are scoped to the native realisations the corpus defines: they exclude neither a
future, differently constructed native valence nor a future construction of a pair-level growth process,
each of which would carry its own derivation obligations.
Asymptotic statements concern real-valued weight sequences on the infinite group $\Heis$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:classification}); statements about the measured integer rank are finite-window statements
in the sense of Proposition~\ref{prop:window}.
At fixed $q$ the span is bounded and necessarily saturates; a joint limit $q \to \infty$, $n = n(q) \to
\infty$ is not claimed, and Remark~\ref{rem:window-scope} records what such a construction would require.

\section{Setting and native definitions}
\label{sec:setting}

\subsection{Substrate}
\label{ssec:substrate}

Let $\Heis$ be the discrete Heisenberg group with generating set $S = \{X^{\pm 1}, Y^{\pm 1}\}$, and let
$S_n$ and $B_n$ denote the BFS sphere and ball of radius $n$ in $\mathrm{Cay}(\Heis, S)$.
The volume growth is polynomial of homogeneous dimension
$\Dhom = 4$~\cite{Bass1972,Guivarch1973}: $|B_n| \asymp n^4$, $|S_n| \asymp n^3$.
Exact BFS enumeration (reproduced here; first computed for the critical-coverage
analysis~\cite{Beau2026ccnote}) gives $s_{22} = 16{,}934$ and $|B_{22}| = 99{,}689$, with fitted exponents
$3.10$ and $3.99$ over $n \in [10, 22]$.
The isoperimetric ratio $|S_n|/|B_n| \approx 3.5/n$ vanishes: this substrate has no expander Cheeger
constant, in contrast with the Ramanujan--LPS family~\cite{LPS88}.

Three regimes must be kept separate: the infinite group $\Heis$; the local pre-wraparound regime of
$\Heisq$, where the finite-quotient balls are isometric to those of $\Heis$; and the saturation regime of
the finite quotient.
The ball-isometry criterion $q > 2\Gamma(n) + 1$, with $\Gamma(n) = \lfloor n^2/4 \rfloor$ the central
extent of $B_n$~\cite{Beau2026ccnote}, controls the second regime: the production windows at
$q \in \{307, 401, 601\}$ (depths $n \le 19$) are entirely pre-wraparound, as are the windows of the larger
primes of the earlier campaign ($2\Gamma(19) + 1 = 181$), while the smallest primes straddle the regimes
($q = 29$ wraps outright, since $|B_{16}| = 27{,}905 > 29^3$).
Every statement below names the regime in which it holds.

\subsection{Capacity, span, and the exact increment identity}
\label{ssec:definitions}

For a Weil block $c$ of $\Heisq$, the pipeline of O12~\cite{Beau2026a16} measures the per-shell block
capacity
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:sigma-def}
  \sigma_c(n) \;=\; \frac{\Delta r(n)}{|S_n|},
\end{equation}
where $\Delta r(n)$ is the incremental Gram--Schmidt span contributed by the fingerprints of shell $S_n$.
We take~\eqref{eq:sigma-def} as the definition of the novelty fraction and define the cumulative span
$r(n) = \sum_{k \le n} \Delta r(k)$.
Three native facts frame everything that follows.

\begin{lemma}[Exact increment identity and unit bound]
  \label{lem:identity}
  $\Delta r(n) = \sigma_c(n)\,|S_n|$ holds identically, $\sigma_c(n) \le 1$, and consequently
  $r(n) \le |B_n|$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  The identity is definition~\eqref{eq:sigma-def} read multiplicatively.
  The $|S_n|$ fingerprint vectors of shell $n$ span at most an $|S_n|$-dimensional increment, so
  $\Delta r(n) \le |S_n|$ and $\sigma_c(n) \le 1$; summing gives $r(n) \le \sum_{k\le n}|S_k| = |B_n|$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The only growing valence]
  \label{rem:valence}
  On the LPS side the dynamical variable of the growth equation is the graph valence
  $p(n)$~\cite{Beau2026a8,Beau2026a10}.
  A Heisenberg Cayley graph has constant degree, so the degree cannot play this role; the cumulative span
  $r(n)$ is the only corpus-native growing candidate, and the notation $p(n) := r(n)$ is used below when the
  contrast with the LPS equation is intended.
\end{remark}

\section{The classification theorem and its rigorous status}
\label{sec:theorem}

\subsection{The abstract theorem: real-valued weights}
\label{ssec:abstract-theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Three-branch span-growth classification, real weights]
  \label{thm:classification}
  Let $(s_k)$ and $(\sigma_k)$ be real-valued sequences with $s_k = \kappa\, k^{D-1}(1 + o(1))$, $D > 1$,
  and $\sigma_k = a\, k^{-\dc}(1 + o(1))$, $a, \dc > 0$.
  Then the weighted sum $r(n) = \sum_{k \le n} \sigma_k\, s_k$ obeys
  \[
    \begin{array}{ll}
      \dc < D: & r(n) = \dfrac{a\kappa}{D-\dc}\, n^{\,D-\dc}\,(1 + o(1)),\\[3mm]
      \dc = D: & r(n) = a\kappa\, (\log n)\,(1 + o(1)),\\[2mm]
      \dc > D: & r_\infty := \lim_n r(n) < \infty
        \quad\text{and}\quad
        r_\infty - r(n) = \dfrac{a\kappa}{\dc-D}\, n^{-(\dc-D)}\,(1 + o(1)).
    \end{array}
  \]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  The summand is $\sigma_k s_k = a\kappa\, k^{(D-1)-\dc}(1+o(1))$.
  For $\dc < D$ the exponent exceeds $-1$, and comparison of the partial sum with
  $\int^n t^{(D-1)-\dc}\,\mathrm{d}t = n^{D-\dc}/(D-\dc)$ gives the first branch; the $o(1)$ factors
  transfer because the tail dominates the sum.
  For $\dc = D$ the summand is $a\kappa\, k^{-1}(1+o(1))$ and the partial sums are
  $a\kappa \log n\,(1+o(1))$.
  For $\dc > D$ the series converges; the tail beyond $n$ is
  $\sum_{k > n} a\kappa\, k^{(D-1)-\dc}(1+o(1)) = \frac{a\kappa}{\dc-D}\, n^{-(\dc-D)}(1+o(1))$ by the same
  integral comparison.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Universality and strictness]
  \label{rem:universality}
  The classification depends only on the pair $(\dc, D)$: all microscopic detail enters through the two
  exponents, and the amplitudes $a, \kappa$ affect only prefactors.
  Saturation begins strictly at $\dc > D$; the boundary case $\dc = D$ grows without bound, logarithmically.
  For $\Heis$, $D = \Dhom = 4$ exactly~\cite{Bass1972,Guivarch1973}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Integer rank: the finite-window formulation}
\label{ssec:window}

\begin{remark}[Integrality obstructs the pointwise hypotheses]
  \label{rem:integrality}
  The measured object is not a real weight: $\Delta r(n)$ is an integer.
  For $\dc > D - 1$ the product $a\kappa\, n^{D-1-\dc}$ tends to zero, so no integer-valued sequence can
  satisfy $\Delta r(n) = \sigma_c(n)|S_n|$ with a pointwise law $\sigma_c(n) = a\,n^{-\dc}(1+o(1))$,
  $a > 0$: eventually either $\Delta r(n) = 0$ exactly --- forcing $\sigma_c(n) = 0$ --- or
  $\Delta r(n) \ge 1$ --- violating the decay.
  This range includes the window-effective values $3 < \dc^{\mathrm{eff}} < 4$ measured on the growth
  branch (Section~\ref{sec:numerics}), not only the saturation branch.
  Theorem~\ref{thm:classification} therefore applies to the integer rank only through the finite-window
  formulation below, never through a pointwise asymptotic hypothesis on $\sigma_c$.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Effective pre-saturation window law]
  \label{prop:window}
  Fix a window $m < k \le n$ in the pre-saturation regime and suppose the finite two-sided bounds
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:window-bounds}
    a_-\, k^{-\dc} \;\le\; \sigma_c(k) \;\le\; a_+\, k^{-\dc},
    \qquad
    \kappa_-\, k^{D-1} \;\le\; |S_k| \;\le\; \kappa_+\, k^{D-1},
  \end{equation}
  with constants $0 < a_- \le a_+$ and $0 < \kappa_- \le \kappa_+$, for all $k$ in the window.
  Then
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:window-law}
    a_-\kappa_- \sum_{k=m+1}^{n} k^{\,D-1-\dc}
    \;\le\; r(n) - r(m) \;\le\;
    a_+\kappa_+ \sum_{k=m+1}^{n} k^{\,D-1-\dc}.
  \end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  Multiply the bounds in~\eqref{eq:window-bounds} through the identity $\Delta r(k) = \sigma_c(k)|S_k|$
  (Lemma~\ref{lem:identity}) and sum over the window.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Scope of every numerical exponent]
  \label{rem:window-scope}
  Statement~\eqref{eq:window-law} is exact, finite, and compatible with integer increments: it requires no
  pointwise power law and no asymptotics, and the branch structure of~\eqref{eq:branches} describes the
  window through the elementary comparison of $\sum_{k \le n} k^{D-1-\dc}$ with $n^{D-\dc}$, $\log n$, or a
  constant.
  Every numerical exponent statement in this note is a window statement in this sense.
  At fixed $q$ the span is bounded ($r(n) \le \dim H_c = q$) and necessarily saturates.
  A joint limit $q \to \infty$, $n = n(q) \to \infty$ would require, beyond the ball-isometry condition
  $q > 2\Gamma(n(q)) + 1$ of Section~\ref{ssec:substrate}, a construction ensuring realizability of the
  integer increments along the sequence --- an average over a growing number of blocks, or an amplitude
  $a(q)$ with a rank margin.
  No such construction is supplied here, and no joint-limit claim is made.
\end{remark}

\section{The valence--exploration no-go}
\label{sec:nogo}

The closure hypothesis of the LPS growth equation --- effective valence proportional to the number of
configurations reached~\cite{Beau2026a8} --- admits exactly two native realisations on a fixed Heisenberg
Cayley graph: the effective valence (new neighbours per frontier vertex), and the cumulative span $r(n)$ of
Remark~\ref{rem:valence}.
This section refutes the closure for both, and for both only: no statement is made about realisations the
corpus does not define.

\begin{corollary}[No valence--exploration proportionality]
  \label{cor:t1}
  Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:classification}, $r(n)/|B_n| \to 0$ for every $\dc > 0$.
  In particular the closure hypothesis of the LPS growth equation --- effective valence proportional to the
  number of configurations reached~\cite{Beau2026a8} --- fails on the Heisenberg substrate for the span
  reading of the valence, in every branch of the classification.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  $|B_n| \asymp n^D$ while $r(n)$ is $O(n^{D-\dc})$, $O(\log n)$, or bounded.
\end{proof}

Corollary~\ref{cor:t1} operates under the real-weight power-law hypotheses.
The following two statements are sharper: the first needs only the window bounds of
Proposition~\ref{prop:window}, the second needs no hypothesis at all.

\begin{proposition}[Proportionality forces a vanishing capacity exponent]
  \label{prop:t1}
  Suppose $r(n) = \lambda\, |B_n|\,(1 + o(1))$ for some $\lambda > 0$ along the cascade, in a regime where
  $|B_n|/n^D$ converges to a positive constant --- as it does on $\Heis$~\cite{Pansu1983}.
  Then the shell-weighted mean capacity over dyadic windows converges to $\lambda$:
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:dyadic-mean}
    \frac{r(2n) - r(n)}{|B_{2n}| - |B_n|}
    \;=\;
    \frac{\sum_{n < k \le 2n} \sigma_c(k)\,|S_k|}{\sum_{n < k \le 2n} |S_k|}
    \;\longrightarrow\; \lambda \;>\; 0 .
  \end{equation}
  Consequently no window bound $\sigma_c(k) \le a_+ k^{-\delta}$ with $\delta > 0$
  (Proposition~\ref{prop:window}) can hold on all sufficiently late dyadic windows: on $(n, 2n]$ the
  left-hand side of~\eqref{eq:dyadic-mean} is at most $a_+ n^{-\delta} \to 0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  The numerator is $r(2n) - r(n) = \lambda(|B_{2n}| - |B_n|) + o(|B_{2n}|)$, and the convergence of
  $|B_n|/n^D$ gives $|B_{2n}| - |B_n| = (1 - 2^{-D})\,|B_{2n}|\,(1 + o(1))$, so the ratio tends to
  $\lambda$.
  The middle expression is the identity $\Delta r = \sigma_c |S_k|$ summed over the window; bounding
  $\sigma_c(k) \le a_+ k^{-\delta} \le a_+ n^{-\delta}$ on $(n, 2n]$ bounds the weighted mean.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[One direction only]
  \label{rem:one-direction}
  The implication runs from proportionality to the vanishing of the decay exponent.
  The converse is false without control of slow variations: $\sigma_c(k) = a/\log k$ has $\delta = 0$ in
  the power-law sense, yet $r(n)/|B_n| \to 0$.
  No converse is claimed or used.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[Fixed-$q$ dimension count]
  \label{cor:dimcount}
  At fixed prime $q$, in every regime, $\Delta r(n) \le \dim H_c = q$~\cite{Beau2026a16} gives
  $r(n) \le q$, while $|B_n| \to |\Heisq| = q^3$.
  Hence $\limsup_n r(n)/|B_n| \le q^{-2}$ and proportionality with any $\lambda > 0$ fails outright ---
  independently of any measurement, window, or power-law hypothesis.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[The bounded-valence realisation]
  \label{rem:bounded-valence}
  The other native realisation of the LPS valence is the effective valence: new neighbours per frontier
  vertex, $|S_{n+1}|/|S_n|$, bounded between $1$ and the degree $|S| = 4$.
  A bounded sequence cannot be proportional to the unbounded $|B_n| \asymp n^4$, so the closure fails for
  this realisation trivially.
  With Corollary~\ref{cor:t1}, Proposition~\ref{prop:t1}, and Corollary~\ref{cor:dimcount} covering the
  span realisation, the no-go covers both corpus realisations --- and only them.
\end{remark}

\section{Native multiplicative coupling and the lost square-root frontier}
\label{sec:coupling}

The increment identity of Lemma~\ref{lem:identity} is the native form of the multiplicative redundancy
insertion of the LPS growth equation~\cite{Beau2026a10}: the novelty fraction multiplies the frontier,
exactly and by definition.
What changes across substrates is what the frontier is a power \emph{of}.

\begin{lemma}[Frontier exponent of the explored volume]
  \label{lem:theta}
  In the infinite-group and pre-wrap\-around regimes, with two-sided constants,
  $|S_n| \asymp |B_n|^{\theta}$ where $\theta = (D-1)/D = \tfrac{3}{4}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  The growth series of $\Heis$ is rational for every finite generating set~\cite{DuchinShapiro2014}, so
  $|S_n|$ is eventually quasi-polynomial; the two-sided ball growth $|B_n| \asymp n^4$~\cite{Bass1972,
  Guivarch1973} pins its degree at $3$, whence $|S_n| \asymp n^3$ and $|S_n| \asymp |B_n|^{3/4}$.
  The exact enumeration gives the window constants: $|S_n|/n^3 \in [1.46, 1.63]$ on $n \in [10, 31]$ and
  $|B_n|/n^4 \in [0.4255, 0.4262]$ on $n \in [15, 31]$.
  The general isoperimetric inequality $|\partial A| \gtrsim |A|^{(D-1)/D}$ for arbitrary finite sets under
  polynomial growth~\cite{CoulhonSaloffCoste1993} is consistent with the statement but is not needed for
  it.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Finite-size frontier exponents]
  \label{rem:theta-window}
  On the measurement windows the effective exponent
  $\mathrm{d}\log|S_n| / \mathrm{d}\log|B_n|$ is $0.78$ on $[10, 19]$ ($0.80$ on $[5, 19]$), decreasing
  monotonically towards $\tfrac34$ ($0.771$ at $n = 19$, $0.763$ at $n = 31$); the window ball exponents
  $D_{\mathrm{eff}} < 4$ of Table~\ref{tab:windows} reflect the same finite-size drift.
\end{remark}

The native growth law is therefore multiplicative against a power of the explored volume,
$\Delta r(n) = \sigma_c(n)\,|S_n| \asymp \sigma_c(n)\,|B_n|^{3/4}$, not against a power of the achieved
span.
Converting $|B_n|^{\theta}$ into a power of $r$ requires a functional $r$--$|B_n|$ law; the linear law is
refuted (Section~\ref{sec:nogo}), and would in any case yield $r^{3/4}$, not the $r^{1/2}$ of the LPS
form.
The next corollary closes the remaining route: no constant can restore the square-root form.

\begin{corollary}[No uniform square-root frontier bound]
  \label{cor:t3}
  Under the sphere-growth hypothesis of Theorem~\ref{thm:classification}, with $\sigma_c(n) > 0$ along the
  cascade, let $D > 2$.
  There is no constant $c$ such that
  $\Delta r(n) \le c\,\sigma_c(n)\sqrt{r(n)}$ holds along the cascade.
  Indeed, by Lemma~\ref{lem:identity} any such constant must satisfy
  \[
    c \;\ge\; \frac{|S_n|}{\sqrt{r(n)}} \;\ge\; \frac{|S_n|}{\sqrt{|B_n|}}
    \;\asymp\; n^{\,D/2 - 1} \;\longrightarrow\; \infty .
  \]
  For $\Heis$ ($D = 4$) the divergence is linear in $n$ (exact-sphere fit: $n^{1.11}$ over $n \in [10,22]$).
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[Why the LPS law does not transpose]
  \label{rem:lps}
  In the LPS derivation~\cite{Beau2026a8,Beau2026a10} the frontier factor $\sqrt{p}$ carries the expander
  isoperimetry of a graph whose valence grows along the cascade, and the additive $\tfrac{1}{2}$
  in~\eqref{eq:lps-law} is precisely the $p$-exponent of that frontier.
  On a polynomial-growth substrate the frontier is the exogenous sphere $|S_n| \sim \kappa n^{D-1}$,
  independent of the achieved span, and carries $p$-exponent zero; Corollary~\ref{cor:t3} shows that no
  uniform constant can restore the square-root form once $D > 2$.
  The Heisenberg capacity-to-growth map is therefore~\eqref{eq:heis-law} on the growth branch, not
  \eqref{eq:lps-law}; neither law implies the other, and each is correct in its own geometric regime.
  Restoring~\eqref{eq:lps-law} for Heisenberg-measured exponents requires a two-substrate construction ---
  LPS dynamics fed by Heisenberg-measured redundancy --- whose identification step is external to both
  substrates and is not supplied here.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[No native carrier for the bounded-flux constant]
  \label{rem:bi}
  In the LPS derivation the bounded-flux constant $\cBI$ shapes the growth equation through a feedback
  loop: the flux bound limits exploration, exploration grows the valence, and the valence sets the frontier
  through which the flux acts~\cite{Beau2026a8}.
  On a fixed Cayley graph the explored volume is autonomous: $|B_n|$ and $|S_n|$ are properties of the
  graph, independent of $\sigma_c$ and of any dynamical bound, and the increment identity contains no
  parameter.
  The constant $\cBI$ can therefore constrain at most the dictionary between cascade rank and physical
  time; it has no carrier in the native growth law.
\end{remark}

\section{Pair and exponent-coordinate obstructions}
\label{sec:obstructions}

The two remaining inputs of the transfer are the substitution of the pair exponent for the block exponent,
and the identification of the capacity exponent with the redundancy exponent of the conversion law.
Neither survives on the native side, in the following corpus-scoped sense.

\begin{remark}[Two distinct exponents named beta]
  \label{rem:beta-category}
  The exponent $\beta_{\mathrm{Heis}} = D - \dc$ of~\eqref{eq:heis-law} is a polynomial growth exponent,
  $r \sim n^{\beta}$.
  The symbol $\bstar$ of the projected-Yukawa mass factorisation acts as an exponential per-shell rate,
  $A(n) = e^{\bstar n}$~\cite{Beau2026pyo}.
  These are different mathematical objects; no statement in this note converts one into the other.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Pair observable versus span increment]
  \label{rem:pair}
  The joint span of a conjugate pair $\{c, q-c\}$ is $r_c + r_{q-c} = 2r_c$, whose increment decays with the
  block exponent $\dc$.
  The pair observable $\sigma_{\mathrm{pair}} = \sigma_c\,\sigma_{q-c}$~\cite{Beau2026a20} is a product with
  exponent $2\dc$ and is not a span increment; feeding $\delta_{\mathrm{pair}} = 2\dc$ into any growth law of
  the present kind would require a product-to-span conversion that is not defined or derived in the corpus.
  No native growth process carrying $\sigma_{\mathrm{pair}}$ --- one whose increment is
  $\sigma_{\mathrm{pair}}|S_n|$, or any other --- is defined or derived in the corpus either; constructing
  one would be a new derivation obligation, and its impossibility is not claimed.
  Theorem~\ref{thm:classification} therefore concerns $\dc$, the block-level exponent.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Exponent coordinates]
  \label{prop:coordinates}
  In the conversion law $\beta = 1/(\alpha + \tfrac12)$ of O6~\cite{Beau2026a10}, $\alpha$ is defined
  against the valence: $R \sim p^{-\alpha}$.
  A capacity exponent $\delta$ is measured against the shell index: $\sigma \sim n^{-\delta}$, in the
  window sense of Proposition~\ref{prop:window}.
  If $p \sim n^{\beta}$ on the window, then $\alpha = \delta/\beta$; the identification $\alpha = \delta$
  holds exactly when $\beta = 1$, the reduced filling model $p(n) = n$ of O7~\cite{Beau2026a11}.
  The reduced model fails natively: on the growth branch the window exponent of the span is
  $D - \dc \ne 1$ throughout the measured range (Table~\ref{tab:windows}).
  Substituting a shell-index exponent for $\alpha$ in~\eqref{eq:lps-law} is therefore a coordinate error
  on this substrate, over and above the failure of the closure it presupposes.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[The separation-of-variables algebra is coordinate-honest]
  \label{rem:selfconsistency}
  The general form behind~\eqref{eq:lps-law} is $\beta = 1/(\alpha + 1 - \theta_p)$, where $\theta_p$ is
  the frontier exponent measured against the growing variable $p$ itself.
  With the native window inputs on the growth branch --- $p = r \sim n^{D-\dc}$, redundancy exponent
  $\alpha_R = \dc/(D-\dc)$, frontier-versus-span exponent $\theta_p = (D-1)/(D-\dc)$ --- it returns
  \[
    \frac{1}{\alpha_R + 1 - \theta_p}
    \;=\; \frac{D-\dc}{\dc + (D-\dc) - (D-1)}
    \;=\; D - \dc
    \;=\; \beta_{\mathrm{Heis}} .
  \]
  The algebra is sound; what fails in the transfer are its inputs --- the closure
  (Section~\ref{sec:nogo}), the frontier exponent (Corollary~\ref{cor:t3}), the redundancy object
  (Remark~\ref{rem:pair}), and the coordinate identification (Proposition~\ref{prop:coordinates}).
\end{remark}

\section{Consequences for the block-level corrections and the exponent estimator}
\label{sec:o14}

\subsection{The shell-growth factor}
\label{ssec:shell-factor}

The block-level analysis of the exact setting~\cite{Beau2026a18,Beau2026a19} applies a shell-growth
correction $D - 1$ and a normalisation term to the measured exponent before insertion
into~\eqref{eq:lps-law}.
On the native side the shell factor needs no correction because it is the boundary term itself: the
increment carries the window exponent $D - 1 - \dc$, and the span the window exponent $D - \dc$, with
$D - 1$ entering additively through $|S_n|$ --- never inside a reciprocal.
A normalisation constant in $n$ (the $q$-normalised capacity variants) rescales the amplitudes $a_\pm$
of~\eqref{eq:window-bounds} and leaves every intra-$q$ window slope unchanged.
The inter-$q$ comparison at a $q$-dependent characteristic depth --- the $\log q / \log n^*$ term
of~\cite{Beau2026a18} --- belongs to the estimator-and-comparison layer across primes and is outside the
present analysis: nothing here derives or refutes it.

\subsection{The shell-index convention}
\label{ssec:estimator}

\begin{proposition}[Estimator inflation under the shifted logarithm]
  \label{prop:estimator}
  Let $\sigma(k) = C\, k^{-\delta}$ exactly on an integer window $W$.
  The ordinary least-squares slope of $\log \sigma$ against $\log(k+1)$ on $W$ is $-\delta'$ with
  \[
    \frac{\delta'}{\delta}
    \;=\; \frac{\mathrm{Cov}_W\!\left(\log k,\, \log(k+1)\right)}{\mathrm{Var}_W\!\left(\log(k+1)\right)}
    \;>\; 1 ;
  \]
  on $W = [3,19]$, $[5,19]$, $[8,19]$ the factor is $1.1321$, $1.1029$, $1.0815$ respectively.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  Ordinary least squares of $y = -\delta \log k$ on the regressor $x = \log(k+1)$ has slope
  $\mathrm{Cov}(x,y)/\mathrm{Var}(x)$; the factors are exact arithmetic on the window.
\end{proof}

Exponents fitted against the shifted logarithm on such windows therefore exceed the plain-logarithm
values --- the convention in which the exponent is defined~\cite{Beau2026a20} --- by $8$--$13\%$.
The gap columns of Section~\ref{sec:numerics} report both conventions for the crossing diagnostic; the
proposition quantifies the conversion between them.
Any value-level use of an exponent fitted under one convention inside a relation stated in the other is
conditional on this conversion, at the fit windows actually used.

\section{Consequences for the admissibility programme}
\label{sec:consequences}

Three objects must be held separate.

\emph{The native law.}
What the measurement substrate imposes is the increment identity, the classification with its two-tier
status (real weights asymptotically, integer rank through finite windows), and the growth-branch map
$\beta_{\mathrm{Heis}} = D - \dc$ --- a polynomial span exponent for the block-level object, with every
numerical value window-scoped.

\emph{The transfer.}
Relation~\eqref{eq:lps-law} applied to Heisenberg-measured exponents rests on inputs that fail natively,
each in its own way: the closure is refuted for both corpus realisations of the valence
(Section~\ref{sec:nogo}); the square-root frontier and with it the additive $\tfrac12$ are
expander-specific, and the bounded-flux constant has no native carrier (Section~\ref{sec:coupling}); the
pair observable carries no defined native growth process, and the coordinate identification
$\alpha = \delta$ holds only in the reduced filling model (Section~\ref{sec:obstructions}).
The measured capacity exponents~\cite{Beau2026a16,Beau2026a20,Beau2026a29}, the shell-alignment
theorem~\cite{Beau2026a26}, the threshold derivation~\cite{Beau2026a27}, and the verticality
result~\cite{Beau2026a28} are untouched: the conditional status of the chain, recorded in the
O-series~\cite{Beau2026a25,Beau2026a28}, is with respect to the fibre structure --- the present results
show that the growth-equation side of the conditions cannot be discharged on the substrate where the
exponents are measured, with the constructions the corpus defines.
Any continued use of~\eqref{eq:lps-law} with Heisenberg-measured exponents therefore relies on a
two-substrate identification --- LPS-type dynamics whose redundancy input is the Heisenberg capacity ---
which must be stated as a hypothesis and defended on its own terms.

\emph{The phenomenological agreement.}
The numerical proximity of $1/(\delta_{\mathrm{pair}} + \tfrac12)$ to the mass-fitted window of the
amplification law is a coincidence check: the present results neither explain nor disturb it, and it
carries no derivational weight on its own.
Its structural derivation on the measurement substrate would require exactly the inputs shown here to
fail; the two-substrate identification above is the only route left open, and it is a hypothesis, not a
result.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

On a single Heisenberg BFS, capacity decay determines span growth completely: the exact increment identity
$\Delta r = \sigma_c |S_n|$, together with the exact polynomial sphere growth of homogeneous dimension
$D = 4$, forces the three-branch classification~\eqref{eq:branches} for real-valued weights, and its exact
finite-window formulation~\eqref{eq:window-law} for the measured integer rank --- the only form in which
the classification reaches the data, since the pointwise power-law hypothesis is unrealizable by integer
increments throughout the measured range $\dc > D - 1$.
The expander-derived conversion law $\beta = 1/(\delta + \tfrac12)$ does not transfer to this substrate:
valence--exploration proportionality fails for both native realisations of the valence --- outright at
fixed $q$ by the dimension count --- the square-root frontier and the additive $\tfrac12$ are
expander-specific, the bounded-flux constant has no native carrier, the pair observable carries no defined
native growth process, and the coordinate identification $\alpha = \delta$ belongs to the reduced filling
model only.
The separation-of-variables algebra itself is coordinate-honest and returns the native growth-branch map
$\beta_{\mathrm{Heis}} = D - \dc$.
On the block-level side, the shell-growth factor $D - 1$ is the native boundary term, additive and outside
any reciprocal, intra-window slopes are invariant under constant normalisations, and the shifted-logarithm
convention inflates fitted exponents by a computable $8$--$13\%$ on the production windows; the inter-$q$
estimator layer is left untouched.
The window-effective exponents of the production data make contact near $q = 211$ and separate strictly for
$q \ge 307$, with a sign stable across all window-shift and knee-trimming variants and both logarithmic
conventions: the contact is a transitional crossing in the accessible data, no current indication forces
$\dc = D$, and the asymptotic value of $\dc$ remains open, as does any joint large-$q$ formulation
(Remark~\ref{rem:window-scope}).

\subsection*{Interpretive outlook}

\textbf{Interpretation, not derived result.}
Read structurally, the classification says that the growth of resolved relational structure on an emergent
substrate is a competition between two exponents only: the geometric opening of the substrate (volume
growth $D$) and the exhaustion of projective novelty (capacity decay $\dc$).
The no-go adds a second structural lesson: a conversion law between capacity and growth is a property of
the substrate's geometry, not of the cascade alone --- carrying such a law across substrates is a
hypothesis about two geometries at once, and on the present evidence the expander law and the polynomial
law are distinct, each correct in its own regime.
On the programme's substrate the measured windows pass through near-balance transitionally --- contact
near $q = 211$, then strict separation on the growth side --- and the regime the crossing leaves behind is
one of slow polynomial growth ($\beta_{\mathrm{Heis}} = D - \dc \approx 0.28$ under the programme's window
estimate): resolved relational structure accumulates far more slowly than the explored configuration space
--- but no structural claim beyond the classification is made here.
